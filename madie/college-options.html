† About the $38,000 figure

What it is: an automatic merit scholarship — $25,000/yr off the $63,120 sticker, landing at ~$38K/yr. No separate application, essay, or interview is required; it's determined solely from cumulative weighted high school GPA at the time of the admission decision. Confirmed directly from Nazareth's own scholarship page, which as of this research was already showing figures for the 2027–28 cycle — the correct application year.

Full tier table: Presidential $25,000/yr (GPA 93–100) · Dean $22,000/yr (GPA 88–92.99) · Founders $20,000/yr (GPA 87.99 and below, no stated floor beneath it).

Two things not yet confirmed:

1. Nazareth grades on a 100-point weighted scale. No published formula converts a standard 4.0/5.0-scale weighted GPA into that 93–100 band, which is the difference between the $25K and $22K tiers. A rough industry rule of thumb (roughly 3.7+ weighted tracking to the 93–100 range on a 100-point scale) is only a generic approximation, not Nazareth's actual formula, so the tier is worth confirming directly with admissions rather than assuming.

2. The "Founders" tier has no floor listed beneath it, which structurally suggests every admitted student receives at least $20K/yr — a common pricing model at smaller private schools with a high sticker price. That's an inference from the table's structure, not a stated guarantee.

Renewal: good for up to 4 years, contingent on maintaining "satisfactory academic progress," a term not defined on Nazareth's page. Whether that aligns with the BFA program's separate 3.0-major-GPA requirement (see Cut/retention risk column) is unconfirmed.

‖ About the $67,600 figure — read this before trusting it

This is the least-confirmed number in the entire table. Treat it as a rough, hedged ballpark, not a real estimate the way Nazareth's or OCU's merit figures are.

What's confirmed: the $87,817 sticker is real, pulled directly from Boston Conservatory at Berklee's own official 2026–27 cost of attendance page. Merit aid is real and automatic through the audition (no separate application) — the school states 92% of enrolled students receive institutional aid, and there's a "Presidential Scholarship" (full tuition + housing + fees) awarded to one student per division (dance/music/theater) per year — a ceiling case, not a realistic expectation.

Where the $67,600 actually comes from: The school's own Net Price Calculator was run anonymously (confirmed by the tool itself: no login, no personal data stored). It returned figures for the 2018–19 academic year — a total cost of attendance of $65,857 and estimated grant aid of $15,250, for a net price of $50,607. That $50,607 number is NOT usable today as-is: it's measuring a school that cost $22K/yr less than it does now. Instead, the discount RATE was extracted ($15,250 ÷ $65,857 ≈ 23%) and applied to today's confirmed $87,817 sticker, producing $87,817 × (1 − 0.23) ≈ $67,600.

Why this could be wrong in either direction: (1) this assumes Boston Conservatory's aid budget has scaled proportionally with its tuition since 2018–19 — not guaranteed, and possible aid has NOT kept pace with 8 years of sticker-price growth, which would mean the real number is higher than $67,600; (2) it's an average across "students similar to you" based on whatever financial/academic inputs were entered into the calculator, not a number specific to any one applicant; (3) it says nothing about audition-based talent scholarship money layered on top, which could push the number either direction depending on how competitive her audition is.

Bottom line: budget for somewhere between this estimate and the full $87,817 sticker until there's a real aid offer in hand. Don't treat $67,600 as a number to plan around financially — treat it as evidence that full sticker is probably not what most families actually pay, without knowing by how much.

Methodology & caveats

Cost figures are sticker cost of attendance (tuition, fees, housing, food, books, personal) for an out-of-state student living on campus, drawn from each school's own financial aid pages where available and cross-checked against IPEDS-sourced aggregators elsewhere. Treat anything without a school's own published figure as directional, not final — confirm with each school's Net Price Calculator once real numbers are needed.

Best case w/ merit only appears where a program offers non-need-based (talent or GPA) aid specifically, since need-based aid isn't assumed in this comparison. Where left blank, either no meaningful merit program was found, or sticker price is the realistic number.

Recognition is a synthesized, subjective 1–10 score — not a formal ranking. It reflects: appearance on repeatedly-cited "top musical theatre program" lists (Michigan, CMU, CCM, NYU, Boston Conservatory, Point Park, Penn State, Syracuse, Pace, OCU) — these lists aren't BFA-specific, which is exactly how a BM program like OCU lands on them alongside mostly-BFA peers — notable current faculty or alumni, and program accreditation (e.g. NAST). A low score does not mean a weak program — several here are simply less nationally visible while still being legitimate, audition-gated training.

Cut/retention risk reflects only what each program documents publicly. Absence of a documented policy is not a guarantee of zero attrition — some programs may counsel students out informally without a written mechanism. The "documented cut risk" stat above counts 8 of the 13 programs (all except Chico, CSUF, UCI, AMDA, and Boston Conservatory), even though UCI's row is also flagged RISK — UCI's mechanism is a candidacy gate (whether a student ever gets into the BFA track after enrolling academically) rather than a post-admission retention/dismissal risk like the other eight, so it's tracked separately rather than folded into the same tally. Chico and CSUF are the only two where no cut policy of any kind was found; AMDA is a third kind of case — its catalog documents only a standard GPA-based warning/probation/dismissal process (under 2.0), with no jury, adjudication, or role-compliance mechanism, so it is flagged clear but is not the same as "no policy." Boston Conservatory is different from both groups: its policies are real but sit behind an authenticated student portal, genuinely unverifiable rather than confirmed-clean.
